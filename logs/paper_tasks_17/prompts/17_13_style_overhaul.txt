## Goal

Bring the entire paper's prose, transitions, and pedagogical rhythm into alignment with the Tong lecture-note style and the Brennan-Hong review style. The paper currently reads like competent AI-generated mathematical exposition. It should read like a physicist wrote it for other physicists. The difference is voice, rhythm, and the ratio of calculation to commentary.

## What the reference documents do that this paper doesn't (yet)

### David Tong's style (qft.md, qhe.md, gt.md):

1. **Opens sections with physical questions, not definitions.** Tong starts with "Why does X happen?" or "What goes wrong when we try Y?" — then derives the answer. Our paper starts sections with "We now define..." or "The purpose of this section is..." The fix: every section opening should pose a question or state a puzzle that the section resolves.

2. **Uses second person and direct address.** "You might worry that..." / "The reader can check..." / "If you've seen the Schwartz derivation, you'll recognize..." — this creates engagement. Our paper uses "one" and passive voice. Shift to "we" and occasional "you" where it's natural.

3. **Includes parenthetical asides that reveal the author's judgment.** "(This is charming but ultimately irrelevant)" / "(More importantly, it is also experimentally wrong)" / "(This is the key step — everything else is bookkeeping)". These tiny editorial intrusions signal a human author who has opinions. Our paper has zero of these.

4. **Physical intuition precedes formal development.** Tong tells you what the answer is going to be, then derives it. Our paper derives first, then interprets. For a QFT reader, the Tong ordering is better: they can follow the derivation because they know where it's going.

5. **Sentence rhythm varies.** Tong uses short punchy sentences between long technical ones. "This is wrong." / "There is a subtlety." / "It works." Our paper has uniform sentence length throughout. Vary it.

6. **Specific forward pointers with payoff.** Tong says "We will see in Section 4 that this innocent-looking factor of 2 is responsible for the mass of the eta-prime." Our paper says "This will be discussed further below." The fix: every forward pointer must name the specific consequence.

### Brennan-Hong style (arXiv-2306.00912v2):

1. **Explicit Ward-identity-level computations shown in full.** They don't skip steps in the operator algebra. Our paper does this well already (ch02, ch05).

2. **Figures that show operator action, not just topology.** Their figures (TopologicalSDOFig, 0FormAction, 1DefectAction, 1formBraiding, LinkingNumbersFig) show *dynamical* processes — an operator deforming, acting on a charged object, linking with another operator. These are more pedagogically useful than static topology diagrams.

3. **Clean theorem/definition/example structure without over-formalization.** They use `\defn{}`, `\expl{}`, `\rmk{}` macros that produce simple bold labels, not heavy boxed environments. Our paper uses `\begin{definition}`, `\begin{theorem}` etc., which is fine — but the prose around the environments should not be formal-logic stiff.

## Specific prose transformations

### Section openings (every chapter, every section)

**Before:** "The purpose of this section is to introduce the concept of X and derive Y."  
**After:** "Why does the Hall conductance come in exact fractions? The answer is a Chern-Simons term."

**Before:** "We now turn to the study of..."  
**After:** [State the question or result directly. No meta-commentary.]

### Transitions between calculations

**Before:** "Having established X, we now proceed to derive Y."  
**After:** "The same coefficient appears in a different context:" or "There is a second consequence of Eq. (N):" or simply start the next calculation — the logical connection should be evident from proximity.

### Interpretive paragraphs after equations

**Before:** "This result is significant because it shows that the observable is topological."  
**After:** "The metric has dropped out. This is a topological invariant — it depends only on the manifold, not on how you measure distances on it." (Concrete → abstract, not abstract → abstract.)

## Process

1. Read each chapter with `qhe.md` or `gt.md` open side-by-side (whichever matches the chapter's content).
2. For each section opening: rewrite to pose a question or state a result.
3. For each transition: kill "having established" / "we now turn" / "the purpose of"; replace with causal connectives or direct statements.
4. For every 3–4 pages: insert at least one parenthetical editorial aside.
5. For every calculation block: verify the interpretive paragraph says something *specific* about why the metric dropped out / why the integer appeared / what physical measurement this controls.
6. Final pass: read aloud. Anything that sounds like a textbook *summary* rather than a textbook *explanation* gets rewritten.

- Every forward pointer names a specific consequence (equation, observable, experiment).
- Reading a random page aloud does not immediately trigger "this sounds like ChatGPT."
- The paper reads as if written by someone who has *opinions* about the physics, not just knowledge of it.

## Key style principles

1. Open sections with PHYSICAL QUESTIONS, not definitions.
   Bad: "We now define the topological susceptibility..."
   Good: "Why is the eta-prime so much heavier than the pion?"

2. Use "we" and occasional "you" -- not "one" or passive voice.

3. Vary sentence rhythm. Short declarative sentences after long derivations.

4. Transitions should be CAUSAL, not meta.
   Bad: "We now turn to the experimental evidence."
   Good: "The prediction is sharp: exp(2pi i/3) for each quasiparticle exchange. Nakamura et al. set out to measure it."

5. Parenthetical asides reveal authorial judgment: "(this is the only place where lattice input is genuinely needed)"
