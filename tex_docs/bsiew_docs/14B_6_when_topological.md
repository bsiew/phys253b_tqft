## Goal

Produce the unified four-category taxonomy of observables that has been foreshadowed by the local "iffiness" subsections in Ch 11 §11.7 and Ch 14 §14.4. This is the paper's synthesis payoff: the reader leaves with a clear organizing principle for which observables are topologically robust and which are not.

## Content

### Four-category table

| Category | Definition | Examples from this paper |
|----------|-----------|------------------------|
| (a) **Strict TQFT observables** | Metric-independent in the exact theory. Computed by functorial quantization. | Wilson-line linking numbers, knot invariants, Hilbert spaces on closed surfaces (Part I Sec 4.4–4.5). |
| (b) **IR topological response** | Universal only below the gap and after decoupling assumptions. Protected by topological order, not by TQFT axioms. | Hall conductance, edge anomaly coefficient, GSD on topology (Ch 11 §11.2–11.4). |
| (c) **Topology-controlled but theory-side** | Defined cleanly in Euclidean QFT or lattice QCD; enter observables only indirectly through derived quantities. | Instanton number, $\chi_t$, $\eta'$ mass via WV (Ch 14 §14.1–14.2). |
| (d) **Topology-inspired but model-dependent** | Topology motivates the quantity, but its value depends on UV completion, mesoscopic details, or model parameters. | Axion couplings (Ch 14 §14.3), monopole production rates (Ch 15 §15.1), interferometric phases in real devices (Ch 11 §11.7). |

### Narrative

1. State each category with its definition and examples.
2. The table is the structural backbone. Discuss each row in one paragraph.
3. Note the gradient: (a) is the purest; (d) is the most physical. Most interesting physics lives in (b) and (c), where topological protection is real but not absolute.
4. Close with the observation that the same mathematics (CS action, characteristic classes, homotopy classification) operates across all four categories — what changes is the relationship between the mathematical quantity and the laboratory observable.
