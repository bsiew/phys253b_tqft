## Goal

Cover the "defect / global-structure" observable family in one place: why magnetic charge sectors are topological, why the underlying theory is not itself topological, and how Wilson/'t Hooft lines probe the global form of the gauge group.

## Content

1. **Dirac monopoles.** Charge quantization $eg = 2\pi n$ as a topological consistency condition: the gauge field on $S^2$ surrounding a monopole must have well-defined transition functions, which requires the first Chern number to be integer. This is $\pi_1(U(1)) = \mathbb{Z}$ in disguise.
2. **'t Hooft–Polyakov monopoles.** Smooth finite-energy solitons in $SU(2) \to U(1)$ breaking. Topology: $\pi_2(G/H) = \pi_2(SU(2)/U(1)) = \pi_2(S^2) = \mathbb{Z}$. Use Part I Sec 2.5.
3. **Contrast.** Dirac: singular, requires string, abelian. 't Hooft–Polyakov: smooth, finite energy, nonabelian. Both: topological charge is a winding number.
4. **Magnetic charge sector $\neq$ topological theory.** The monopole itself lives in a dynamical Yang-Mills theory with local degrees of freedom. The topological content is the charge quantization and the sector label, not the full dynamics.
5. **Wilson and 't Hooft lines.** Wilson line $W_R(\gamma) = \text{tr}_R \, \mathcal{P} \exp(\oint_\gamma A)$ probes the electric sector; 't Hooft line probes the magnetic sector. Their mutual noncommutativity probes the global form of the gauge group ($SU(2)$ vs $SO(3)$: which lines are allowed?).
6. **Preview of generalized symmetries.** Wilson/'t Hooft lines generate 1-form symmetries in the sense of Gaiotto-Kapustin-Seiberg-Willett 2015. One paragraph, cite for details.
7. **Experimental status.** No magnetic monopole observed. PDG bounds. Collider and cosmic-ray searches. Fairbairn et al. 2021 as modern bridge between theory and experiment.
