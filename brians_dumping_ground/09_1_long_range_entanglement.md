## Goal

Introduce topological order as the modern phase-of-matter concept characterized by long-range entanglement of the ground state, and contrast explicitly with the Landau symmetry-breaking paradigm.

## Content

1. **Symmetry breaking paradigm**, brief review. Order parameter $\langle \phi \rangle \neq 0$ distinguishes phases; local probes distinguish them.
2. **What fails.** Gapped phases exist that have no local order parameter but are still distinct from the trivial phase (and from each other). These are topologically ordered.
3. **Long-range entanglement.** A gapped ground state is topologically ordered if it cannot be deformed to a product state by any finite-depth local unitary. Contrast with symmetry-broken states, which can be.
4. **Experimental signatures.** Ground-state degeneracy on nontrivial manifolds, anyonic excitations, topological entanglement entropy $\gamma = \log \mathcal{D}$.
5. Foreshadow: the rest of Chapter 9 makes these signatures precise via the toric code example; Chapter 10 develops anyon language; Chapter 11 connects to FQH.
